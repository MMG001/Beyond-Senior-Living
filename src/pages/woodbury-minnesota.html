History of Woodbury, Minnesota

Woodbury, Minnesota, is a Washington County community in the eastern Minneapolis–Saint Paul metropolitan area. Its history traces the transformation of a farming township into a suburban, commercial and recreational center. According to the city’s historical account, settlers arrived in 1844, and the community organized as a township in 1858. Originally called Red Rock, it became Woodbury in 1859 after state lawmakers identified another Minnesota township with the same name. The new name honored Levi Woodbury of New Hampshire, a judge and public official who was a friend of the first town board chairman.

Woodbury’s early population included immigrants from Germany, Ireland, Sweden, Switzerland, Scotland and Denmark. Agriculture shaped the community before suburban development accelerated in the 1960s. In 1967, residents approved village incorporation and a mayor-council government, and the community developed its first comprehensive land-use plan. These milestones help explain the relationship between Woodbury’s agricultural heritage and its present-day neighborhoods, business districts, parks and public facilities.

History of Woodbury

Several people and dates provide a useful timeline of Woodbury’s development. William Middleton owned the small house in which the community’s first school opened in 1855. Township organization followed in 1858, and the Woodbury name was adopted in 1859. In 1876, the community built its first town hall, and James Middleton was elected to represent the district in the state legislature. A new municipal building followed in 1975 as public services expanded.

  • 1844: Settlers arrive
  • 1855: First school opens in William Middleton’s house
  • 1858: Township organized (as Red Rock)
  • 1859: Renamed Woodbury, after Levi Woodbury
  • 1876: First town hall built; James Middleton elected to the state legislature
  • 1967: Village incorporation and mayor-council government approved
  • 1975: New municipal building
  • October 1983: Woodbury Heritage Society founded

The Woodbury Heritage House offers a physical connection to this early history. The Woodbury Heritage Society dates the building to approximately 1870, when it was constructed as an addition to Frederick Raths’s log cabin home. The Heritage Society itself was founded in October 1983 to document and preserve the community’s past. Its collections, research services and historic-property work help residents understand the families and agricultural landscape that preceded modern Woodbury.

Parks & Outdoors

Woodbury’s parks offer destinations for trail recreation, nature observation, cycling, golf and organized sports. Carver Lake Park includes access to a 5.5-mile off-road cycling trail. The city also maintains a beach-information page; visitors should check posted water-quality notices before planning water activities.

Tamarack Nature Preserve protects a wetland and woodland landscape with approximately two miles of trails. The city describes its wetland as a rich fen and notes that the preserve contains one of Minnesota’s southernmost stands of tamarack trees. Powers Lake Park and Powers Lake East provide additional destinations for exploring Woodbury’s lake landscapes. The sports-center campus also includes Madison’s Place Playground and a splash pad. For local sports fans, the East Ridge Raptors and Woodbury Royals are the community’s recognizable high-school teams.
